## The problem

B2B data businesses often sell several interconnected services to different buyer groups. A sales team may need prospect lists; a marketing manager may need technographic targeting; another organisation may need ongoing database maintenance or outreach support. A successful website must explain these differences quickly without forcing every visitor into a general sales conversation.

The **earlier homepage shown in the saved before-and-after comparison** uses a large generic hero image, a short brand headline and a row of four service tiles. Those elements identify the business but give limited immediate explanation of how its intelligence offering relates to the buyer's goals.

The redesign presentation addresses this communication gap with a more explicit proposition, an expanded service overview, data-themed visual language and distinct enquiry calls to action. This describes the comparison shown in the saved artwork, not an independently audited conversion problem.

## How the Tech Conversion website project was presented

The following points describe what is **visible in the saved design comparison** and what aligns with the company's published service categories. They do not claim a particular unpublished implementation process or a confirmed rollout of every proposed element.

1. **Lead with the core business proposition.** The proposed hero makes “Data Intelligence. Digital Growth. Technology Solutions.” the central message, rather than relying on a generic aspirational headline.
2. **Connect services to business outcomes.** The presentation explains targeted data, database quality, qualified leads and digital solutions as parts of a coherent growth offering.
3. **Give key services individual space.** The proposed service grid distinguishes list management, technographics, digital marketing, database management, MSP data insights and custom data solutions.
4. **Make sampling and enquiries easier to discover.** A prominent *Request a Sample* action appears in the proposed header and hero, alongside an *Explore Services* route.
5. **Use a visual language appropriate to data intelligence.** Charts, dashboard elements and a global-network graphic communicate the subject matter more directly than a generic team photograph.
6. **Improve the scan path.** Clear headings, grouped navigation, short descriptions and structured cards help visitors find the relevant capability faster.
7. **Show a responsive presentation direction.** A second saved case-study image includes a phone and desktop mockup. This supports a responsive design intention, but does not independently validate every live breakpoint.
8. **Treat figures responsibly.** Numbers and claims visible in design-stage mockups—including geographic reach and data-accuracy statistics—must not be presented as verified business results or website-performance improvements.

## Standards the website should be reviewed against

The following are **review criteria**, not statements that an independent audit has been completed.

- [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals): review loading speed, responsiveness and layout stability for marketing and service pages.
- [WCAG 2.2 Level AA](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): review readable contrasts, keyboard support, headings, focus states, accessible forms and meaningful alt text.
- [Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials): review indexable service content, page titles, site structure and helpful buyer-oriented explanations.
- [Schema.org](https://schema.org/): consider accurate organisation, service and breadcrumb markup when supported by live content.
- **Data privacy and marketing compliance:** assess the organisation's applicable requirements for B2B data sourcing, contact preferences, consent, retention and email marketing in the jurisdictions it serves.
- **Published claims:** confirm dataset counts, accuracy percentages, delivery figures, testimonials and customer-performance statements before treating them as audited facts.
